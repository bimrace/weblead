Public & government buildings

What public-sector projects need from the model

Many hands, one record

A public building passes through a client body, design consultants, contractors, auditors and a facilities team. Each needs to understand what was decided and why. That only works when the coordinated model, the drawings and the revision register agree with each other — so we keep them as one source rather than three parallel documents.

Every change has to be traceable

Public projects are reviewed, re-scoped and audited. A clash that moved a duct, a design change that moved a riser, a quantity that changed between revisions — each needs a record of what changed, when and on whose instruction. Revision control and change reports are part of the deliverable, not an afterthought.

Handover outlives the project

The organisation that runs the building is usually not the one that built it. Asset registers, equipment schedules and a clear handover note are what let a facilities team find and maintain plant years later.

Coordination and documentation priorities

What we prioritise on public and government buildings

These are the practical priorities that make a public-sector BIM deliverable useful to the client body, the contractor and the people who maintain the building.

PriorityCoordinated documentation
Plans, sections, risers and schedules derived from one coordinated model, so drawings and model never disagree.
PriorityTraceable revisions
Drawing and model revision registers, clash issue logs and quantity change reports that show what changed, when and why.
PriorityMultidisciplinary collaboration
Architectural, structural and MEP models federated on shared coordinates, with clash reports the whole team can act on.
PriorityMaintainable asset information
Equipment and asset registers, and COBie-style data where the client specifies it, prepared for the facilities team.
PriorityClearly defined deliverables
LOD/LOI, formats, exclusions and handover contents agreed in the proposal — the deliverable list is a document, not an assumption.

How an engagement runs

Five steps from enquiry to handover

The same sequence on every project, so you know at each point what you will receive next and what we need from you.

01

Enquiry and inputs

You send the scope, stage and the drawings or models you have. We confirm what is usable, what is missing and which services the project actually needs.

02

Scope and proposal

We agree the deliverables, the level of model detail (LOD/LOI — how much geometry and data each element carries), file formats, programme and what is excluded.

03

Modelling and coordination

Revit models are built from your design information and checked against architecture, structure and each other. AI-assisted checking groups the issues; engineers resolve them.

04

QA/QC and issue

Every issue passes model QA/QC before it leaves us. Drawings, schedules, quantities and clash reports are issued under revision control.

05

Handover

Native models, drawings, registers and a handover note listing files, assumptions and anything left open — so the building owner can actually use what they receive.

Scope boundary

What a public-sector appointment does not include

Stated at enquiry stage, because a boundary discovered in month three is a dispute and the same boundary stated now is just information.

  • Statutory approvals, authority submissions and professional sign-off remain with the project's appointed designers and licensed professionals.
  • Engineering design and calculations are provided only when included in the agreed scope and supported by appropriately qualified personnel.
  • We do not claim public-sector framework appointments, security clearances or government accreditations. Where a project requires them, tell us at enquiry stage.
  • As-built accuracy depends on verified site information and a defined verification responsibility.

FAQ

Questions we are asked at enquiry stage

Do you work on government and public-sector buildings?

Yes — public and government buildings are a core sector in our service catalogue, across all four MEP disciplines. This page describes the scope and workflow; it does not claim specific public-sector contracts, because we publish no project history until a client releases it.

Can you work to our public-sector BIM standards and templates?

Yes. We work to the client's BIM execution plan, templates, naming and exchange requirements where they exist, and agree them at the start where they do not.

What does 'traceable revisions' mean in practice?

Every issue of the model and drawings is logged in a revision register, clash issues are tracked to closure, and quantity changes between revisions are reported. Anyone reviewing the project later can see what changed and when.

Do you hold security clearances?

Not that we publish here. If a project requires cleared personnel or specific data-handling arrangements, say so in your enquiry and we will confirm what is possible before we commit.

What will the facilities team receive at handover?

Native models, drawings, equipment and asset registers, and a handover note listing files, assumptions and open items. The exact contents are agreed in the proposal.
