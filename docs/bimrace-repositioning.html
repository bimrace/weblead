02

Audit

What the old site got right, and its one structural flaw

The starting point was not a bad website. It was a good consultancy website describing a company that had quietly stopped being a consultancy.

What worked and was kept

  • Radical honesty. An explicit "What we will not claim" section — no client logos, no project counts, no invented certifications. This is rarer than it should be and it became the spine of the whole rebuild rather than a section on the About page.
  • Genuine engineering specificity. Copy that named EIR, BEP, MIDP, LOIN and suitability codes. Buyers in this sector read that as competence within seconds.
  • The technical build. A Python generator with one source of truth for chrome, zero framework, zero runtime dependencies, hand-drawn isometric SVG, working reduced-motion and focus states. The rebuild extended this rather than replacing it.
  • The lead engine behind it. Supabase with RLS, trigger-driven scoring and routing as data rather than code, and a 33-assertion security test suite. Untouched.

What was wrong

Structural

The differentiator was pillar 06

"Engineering Intelligence" appeared as the sixth of six capability cards, below "Construction Support". The single thing separating BIMRACE from a thousand MEP BIM bureaus was positioned as an afterthought.

Category

Competing in a commodity race

"Digital engineering for complex building projects" places the firm in a category priced per hour against offshore drafting. Nothing in the headline suggested a different kind of company.

Evidence

Automation asserted, never shown

"Automation applied where it pays" was a claim with no demonstration anywhere on the site. A visitor had no way to distinguish it from every competitor's identical sentence.

Visual

A light corporate surface

White ground, blue-grey palette, ISO-19650 language. Correct for a consultancy, wrong for a company claiming to build engineering software. The medium contradicted the message.

Information

Binary honesty

Capabilities were either stated flatly or hedged in a paragraph of prose. There was no mechanism letting a reader see, line by line, what is real today versus what is being built.

Conversion

One generic path

"Start an enquiry" served every visitor identically, whether they wanted a drafting quote or wanted to know whether the AI claims were real.

03

Positioning

Engineering Intelligence. Built Around BIM.

The category is AI Engineering Intelligence. The proof that it is not a rebranding exercise is the four-layer thesis, which the site repeats in the hero, the footer and the structure of every page.

Layer 01Data
BIM is the engineering data layer. Structured engineering context a machine can read — not a 3D picture with data attached.
Layer 02Intelligence
AI is the intelligence layer. Reasoning over that context against engineering rules written by people who have sized the systems they govern.
Layer 03Execution
Automation is the execution layer. Running what has been validated — drafting, never committing.
Layer 04Validation
Engineering expertise is the validation layer. A named person who is accountable, which is the component we have no intention of automating.

The counter-positioning that makes it defensible

Two competitor archetypes are named directly in the homepage copy: the BIM bureau that sells layer one, and the AI vendor that sells layers two and three "and has never sized a duct". The claim is not that BIMRACE is better at either. It is that only an engineering practice can be accountable for all four — which is a claim a bureau and a software vendor structurally cannot make.

The honesty system is the differentiator, not a disclaimer. Four states — Live In development Roadmap Demo data — appear beside individual line items, not sections, because a section-level badge is how vague claims hide. The legend is published at platform.html#status with an explicit invitation to audit the site against it.

This costs some early enthusiasm and buys the technical conversation, which is where this business is actually won. It also inoculates against the obvious objection to any AEC company using the word AI in 2026.

05

Homepage

Thirteen sections, each doing one job

The sequence is an argument: establish the category, name the pain, present the model, prove each layer, then show the thing working.

  1. Hero — the thesis

    Headline, the four-layer block, and a live analysis pass over an isometric MEP model with one clearance failure flagged for review. Readouts are labelled as illustrative in two places.

  2. The problem

    Four named failure modes: the model knows but cannot answer; rules live outside the thing they govern; checking scales with headcount; decisions leave no readable trace.

  3. The approach + status legend

    The four layers restated, with the capability legend placed early rather than buried — the trust anchor for everything below.

  4. Intelligence stack

    Five instrument rows with per-layer status and discipline-coloured tag chips, joined by a repeated connector reading "DATA FLOWS UP · ACCOUNTABILITY FLOWS DOWN".

  5. AI engineering agents

    The five-beat reasoning chain, then eight agents with Input / Reasoning / Output stated for each. Four in development, four roadmap.

  6. What we automate

    Six workflow cards with the step sequence and the actual output artefact. Five are live.

  7. BIM intelligence

    "Your BIM model is an engineering database", with the data-layer diagram and the uncomfortable corollary that a badly authored model cannot be rescued by an intelligence layer.

  8. Engineering

    Three service categories — Engineering, BIM, Intelligence — with a status badge on every one of the 24 line items.

  9. Digital twin

    Composition diagram, roadmap-flagged, opening with "A 3D model on a web page is not a digital twin."

  10. Case studies

    The five-part anatomy published in advance, with the commitment that failures appear alongside successes.

  11. Engineering console

    Three scripted scenarios with a streaming analysis log. Every run ends by holding for engineering review.

  12. Why BIMRACE

    Six claims that can be tested at enquiry stage, each badged.

  13. Final CTA

    "Build the next generation of engineering workflows."

06

Content

Copy rules, and the sentences that carry the strategy

The governing rule: a claim that names artefacts can be checked; a claim that names a feeling cannot. Every workflow description on the site names a system assignment, a parameter, a rule or an element ID.

Banned constructions

  • "AI-powered", "AI-driven", "powered by AI" as standalone modifiers. AI is demonstrated through architecture, workflows and interfaces, not adjectives.
  • "Cutting-edge", "innovative", "world-class", "seamless", "revolutionise".
  • Any percentage, multiplier or time saving without a named baseline and method.
  • "Learn more", "Discover", "Click here".

Lines doing structural work

Hero

"AI-assisted engineering workflows that connect BIM models, MEP design, calculations, engineering rules and project data into one system — with a named engineer accountable at the end of every one of them."

Agents

"Specialised workflows, not a chatbot with a hard hat." Followed by: "None of these run unattended. Where an agent exists it reads, tests and drafts."

Clash

"Clash detection produces a number. Clash intelligence produces a decision — what actually conflicts, why, and whose model has to move."

Twin

"What we will not do is sell you a model viewer and call it a twin."

Projects

"A case study library with no failures in it is a brochure."

Accountability

"AI accelerates engineering. Engineers remain accountable for engineering decisions." Not a disclaimer — a structural constraint that shapes what gets built.

Where automation stops — published as six "never" statements

Writes to a live model · closes an issue · issues a deliverable · changes a status code · settles an engineering judgement · trains on a client's model. The page states the trade explicitly: these constraints make the workflows slower than a fully autonomous system and make the output defensible, "which is the only state in which an engineering deliverable has value".

The console

Three scripted scenarios — fire protection coordination, chilled water system, model QA pass — each with a metric panel and a streaming log colour-coded RUN / OK / WARN / RISK. The findings are specific enough to be arguable ("CLR-014 — 42mm clearance to AHU-04 supply duct, 150mm required"), which is the point.

Simulation is disclosed in four places. A Demo data badge in the panel bar, "SIMULATED SESSION — NO MODEL IS CONNECTED TO THIS PAGE" and "FIGURES ARE FABRICATED FOR ILLUSTRATION" in the footer strip, and a caption below. Every scenario's final log line is the system holding for engineering review — the design, stated as such rather than as a limitation of the demo.

09

Design

Visual system and UX changes

A light drafting sheet. The rule applied throughout: nothing is decorative that could be informative.

GroundSurfaces
#F2F5F8 paper — a cool neutral biased a touch toward the teal accent — with four surface steps up to white and hairlines at 13/21/33% alpha. A blueprint grid appears as a masked background on the hero, page headers and CTA only.
ColourMeaning, not decoration
One signal teal #0B7C74 for live data and the primary action. Four discipline hues carried from MEP coordination drawings — mechanical teal, electrical amber #97620A, plumbing indigo #3A56CF, fire protection violet #8A3FBF — plus risk red, all at ink densities that hold up as hairline strokes and small type on paper. Amber doubles as the human-in-the-loop colour: every human stage in the pipeline diagram is amber.
ContrastWCAG AA throughout
The neutral ramp was solved against paper rather than eyeballed. All four text steps clear 4.5:1 — text-4 carries 9px mono labels and had been failing at 3.10:1, now 4.62:1 — as does every discipline hue and every darkened type variant. This is the one measurable respect in which the light build beats the dark one it replaced.
TypeTwo faces
IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Mono. Mono carries every label, readout, status badge and log line — it signals instrument rather than brochure. Weights trimmed to 400/500/600, dropping two font files from the request.
MotionExplains a system
A sweep is a model being read. A travelling dot is data on a named route. A log line is a workflow step. Nothing moves for atmosphere, and everything stops under prefers-reduced-motion.
RejectedDeliberately absent
Stock construction photography, blue corporate gradients, robot and brain graphics, glassmorphism, neon, particle fields, generic chatbot bubbles.

UX changes that matter

  • The status badge appears in the navigation dropdown itself, so the honesty system is visible before a visitor commits to a click.
  • The hero's flagged clearance failure survives the mobile crop; only its leader line and caption step aside, so the point of the drawing is never lost.
  • Stack layers respond to keyboard focus, not only hover.
  • The contact page offers a working session under NDA as an alternative to a proposal, and states plainly that roadmap items cannot be demonstrated.

SEO — competing for the emerging category

Titles and descriptions were rewritten around AI BIM, BIM automation, Revit automation, AI MEP engineering, engineering automation, BIM intelligence, AEC AI, BIM AI agents and digital twin engineering — carried by pages that genuinely address each, rather than stuffed. The four new pages are the substantive play: they target queries the old site had no page for. Sitemap priorities were reweighted so platform, engineering and automation sit at 0.9.

Roadmap

What to do next

P0 blocks publication. P1 is the first fortnight. P2 and P3 are where the positioning either becomes true or stays a website.

P0Before publishing
Audit the register above against reality. Then add the /capabilities.html → /engineering.html 301 in the Amplify console, since netlify.toml is not read there. Then confirm the enquiry form still writes to crm.enquiry_submissions against the live Supabase project — the field contract was preserved and inspected, but it has not been tested end-to-end with real credentials.
P1First two weeks
Replace the founder portrait's Supabase-hosted URL with a local asset — an external image request on every About view is both a performance and a privacy leak. Regenerate the favicon set for the dark identity. Re-run Lighthouse against the deployed site. Get one client to release a case study; it is worth more than any further design work.
P2One to three months
Build Insights properly or not at all — three substantive technical pieces (a real rule set explained, a false-positive analysis, a workflow post-mortem) beat twenty SEO posts. Add per-agent detail pages as the agents mature. Consider a gated technical deep-dive as a lead magnet aimed at BIM managers rather than procurement.
P3Beyond
Connect the console to a real read-only model service so the demonstration becomes a demonstration. That single change moves AI positioning from 8 to 10 and is the only thing on this list that does. Everything else is presentation; that is product.

Open risks

  • The register is a promise. The site's credibility now rests on it being accurate. That is the intended design, and it is a liability if the audit in P0 is skipped.
  • Light-only. The site shipped dark first and was re-themed light on request. It is a single-theme commitment with no dark fallback — but every colour is now a token with no literals outside the :root block, so reversing it or adding a prefers-color-scheme variant is a change to one block, not to 900 lines.
  • The console is fiction. Disclosed four times, but a hostile reader can still frame it as a mock-up. P3 removes the objection entirely.
  • Verification was headless. Nesting, links, anchors, alt text and overflow were measured programmatically and every page was reviewed by screenshot in Edge. The site has not been opened in Safari or Firefox, on a real phone, or with a screen reader.
